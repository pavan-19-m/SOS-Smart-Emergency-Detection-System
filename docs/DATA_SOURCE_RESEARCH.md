## Source findings and limits

The crash CSV is suitable for **station-level recorded counts and within-dataset count ranking**, not street-level map markers, time-of-day/month trends, density estimation, exposure-adjusted rates, or exact nearby distances. Station boundary geometry and traffic exposure are absent. Fatal crash counts and fatalities are separate measures and must not be conflated.

The Bengaluru 2023 crime CSV supports citywide category totals and reported/detected comparisons over 2021–2023. It does not support city-area hotspots, crime timing, or a location-aware crime map. The table can contain crime classes such as robbery/theft/burglary if the actual rows do; the normalization preserves publisher labels and never invents categories. Snatching availability must be determined from actual rows after download.

Karnataka 2024/2025 aggregates may support a separate state/district trend report after download, but they cannot be assigned to Bengaluru streets. Counts must not be compared across 2023 IPC-based Bengaluru rows and later IPC/BNS/SLL totals without compatible definitions and geography.

BIGRS report findings are contextual, sourced analysis rather than computed CSV output: BTP recorded 894 fatal crashes and 921 fatalities in 2023; 324 fatal crashes were in North, 205 West, 198 South, and 167 East. The report says fatalities increased 19% over 2022, 59% of fatalities were motorcyclists and 31% pedestrians, and close to 90% involved those two groups. It identifies high-risk fatality corridors along Ballari Road (North), Magadi/Mysuru Roads (West), Hosur Road/ORR (South), and Old Madras/Whitefield Main Roads (East). These are the report's own scope and definitions, not values derived from the station total CSV. Named location and corridor tables cover 2021–2023; report maps are not digitized into GPS points.

## Download and completeness status

Automated direct download was attempted from the publisher-hosted URLs. DNS/network access was initially unavailable in the sandbox; after network access was approved, the publisher served a Cloudflare JavaScript challenge instead of the CSV. Therefore actual CSV downloads, full-record inspection, record counts, duplicate/missingness reports, and the report PDF download remain incomplete. OpenCity's catalogued schema and file sizes are metadata, not a substitute for inspecting the actual file.
